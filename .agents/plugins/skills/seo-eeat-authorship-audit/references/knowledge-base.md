# Knowledge base : read before grading evidence_basis on a finding

Full detail lives in `EEAT and Authorship/Authorship-Algorithm-EEAT-Research-Companion.html`
(cross-verified against 10 sources). This file is the condensed version: just what a
finding-writer needs to grade a claim correctly.

## E-E-A-T is not a ranking factor : it's a rating framework
Google's own helpful-content page states this directly: E-E-A-T isn't itself a
specific ranking factor; ranking systems use a mix of signals that correlate with
good E-E-A-T, and human Quality Rater feedback refines those systems over time
rather than scoring pages live. Never write a finding that implies E-E-A-T is
computed and applied to a page directly : write it as "this signal is part of what
raters/systems associate with trustworthy content."

## Real authorship-patent history (grounds the Authoritativeness rules)
Three separate, dormant Google patent families, 2011 to 2015 : not one connected
system, and no source confirms any is still an active ranking input today:
1. `US2013/0066970` / `/0066971` : rel=author/Google+ markup (the actual public
   feature, discontinued 2014).
2. `US2012/0265755` : contributor reputation scored by peer review.
3. `US8,825,698` → `US9,165,030` : "authoritative user" identification for search
   results (Bill Slawski's own label "Authoritative Rank" is editorial, not
   Google's term).
Cite these as **historical grounding for why the concept exists**, never as "this
patent is currently live in ranking."

## Koray Tugberk Gubur's "Algorithmic Authorship" : evidence-thin, label it so
His real, verifiable definition (from `holisticseo.digital`): a deliberate writing
discipline : fixed sentence/paragraph structures, transition ("bridge") words,
discourse patterns : meant to produce a detectable writing signature. Checked in
detail:
- The "statistical signature" claim (Medium article) resolves to spotting a
  **repeated signature phrase**, then searching for it : phrase-matching, not a
  measurable feature set. No sentence-length, vocabulary-diversity, or syntax
  metric anywhere in his own material.
- His "algorithmic authorship" writing-rules page names the right categories
  (sentence structure, bridge words, "subcategorization frames") but gives no
  actual word list or template : real specifics are gated behind a paid course
  and a forthcoming book.
- The one genuinely specific, mechanistic "writing → author signature" technique
  found anywhere in this research is a real forensic-linguistics patent
  (character-10-gram frequency + cosine similarity + z-test voting,
  `US20210035065A1`) : but it belongs to Duquesne University, has nothing to do
  with Google or SEO, and was misattributed as Google-related by at least one
  source the user supplied. If a finding wants to reference "how a stylometric
  signature could actually be built," cite this patent explicitly as "real
  forensic-linguistics technique, not a Google mechanism" : never blur the two.

**Every rule sourced `SRC-GUBUR` carries `evidence_basis=Interpretation`.** Report
it as "we recommend, based on a practitioner framework" : never "Google requires."

## "200 authorship rules" : does not exist, don't reference it
If a user or a page's own marketing copy claims to follow "Koray's 200 authorship
rules" or similar, don't validate the number. After 8 search-query variations
across the research companion's build, no page by Gubur or anyone else states this
figure : it traces to a search engine's own AI-generated summary that fabricated it
from a page containing no rule count at all. Real third-party derivative counts
exist (12 / 41 / 55, from three different agencies), none traceable to a Gubur
original. Treat "200 rules" the same as an unverifiable marketing claim.
